[[steps]]
heading = "A level tested twice"
text = "The bounce play trades a zone price has **reacted to at least twice** (Lesson 24). A floor tested twice is a **double bottom**; a ceiling, a **double top**. CME Group describes these as a market failing twice to push past the same price."
why = "CME Group's reversal lesson: on its second try the market couldn't make a lower low (or a higher high), which suggests it may be running out of momentum in that direction."

[[steps]]
heading = "Where it fits"
text = "It works best in a **range** (Lesson 25), bouncing between floor and ceiling, or at a major zone in the higher-timeframe direction. Buying a bounce off support in an H4 downtrend fights the trend: lower odds, so beginners skip it."
why = "Range edges are where the bounce play lives. In a strong trend, the pullback play usually fits better."

[[steps]]
heading = "Confirmation, then the trade"
text = "Same discipline as the pullback: wait for a **confirmation candle** at the zone to **close**, put the stop beyond its wick and the zone, and aim for the next zone, often the middle or the other side of the range. At least twice the risk, or skip."
why = "In a range, a sensible first target is the middle of the range: the other edge is the next resistance, and price often stalls halfway."

[[steps]]
heading = "When levels break"
text = "Levels don't hold forever: each test uses up some of the orders waiting there. If a candle **closes beyond** the zone, the bounce idea is dead. The stop takes you out with a planned loss, and the broken level may flip (Lesson 24)."
why = "That's why the stop belongs beyond the zone, not inside it: if price only pokes in and comes back, you stay in; if it closes beyond, you're out."

[[steps]]
heading = "Pullback or bounce?"
text = "The **pullback** trades with a trend after it pauses. The **bounce** trades off a proven level, often in a range. Both are Classic Price Action: a confirmation candle at a level, with the trend or the range, and a stop."
why = "Your Trading Plan names which plays you take. Stage 8 writes it down; Stage 6 adds Fibonacci and confluence to both."
