[[steps]]
heading = "When two big centres trade at once"
text = "From about **8 am to noon New York time**, London and New York are both open. This **overlap** is usually gold's busiest window: the most buyers and sellers, so moves are cleaner and the spread is usually at its tightest."
widget = "session_clock"
why = "London's session runs to 5 pm London time, which is noon in New York for most of the year. During the few weeks when the US and UK clocks change on different dates, the overlap is an hour longer or shorter. The session clock always shows the real one."

[[steps]]
heading = "Why busier is better for beginners"
text = "With more traders, your order fills closer to the price you see, and price moves more cleanly. In quiet hours the spread can widen and price can drift or jump on small orders. This course asks you to learn and practise in the overlap first."
why = "Traders call this **liquidity**: how easily you can buy or sell without moving the price. You'll choose your allowed sessions in your Trading Plan, and the journal checks every Demo Trade against them."

[[steps]]
heading = "Busy also means news"
text = "Big US reports such as inflation (**CPI**) and jobs (**NFP**) come out at **8:30 am New York time**, inside the overlap. Gold can move several dollars in seconds. In the next lesson you'll learn to step aside for those few minutes."
why = "The Fed's interest-rate decisions are announced at 2:00 pm New York time, after the overlap, with a press conference at about 2:30 pm. You'll learn what each report means in Stage 3."

[[steps]]
heading = "The Asian range"
text = "Before London opens, note the **highest and lowest price of the Asian session**. When London arrives with more traders, price often tests one of those edges. They're the first two levels you'll write in your Pre-Session Checklist."
why = "A test isn't a promise: price may break through the level or turn away from it. The levels tell you where to pay attention, not what will happen."

[[steps]]

why = "Your device's time zone decides your local times. If you travel, the clock follows your device."
