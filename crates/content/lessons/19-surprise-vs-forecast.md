[[steps]]
heading = "Previous, forecast, actual"
text = "Every release on the calendar shows three numbers: **previous** (last time), **forecast** (what economists expect) and **actual** (what came out). Markets have already priced in the forecast. What moves price is the **surprise**: actual compared with forecast."
why = "The forecast is usually the middle of many economists' estimates, collected before the release. Calendars sometimes call it the consensus."

[[steps]]
heading = "Hot or soft?"
text = "A **hot** number points to a stronger economy or higher inflation than expected: CPI above forecast, more jobs than forecast. A **soft** number is the opposite. Careful with unemployment: a **higher** rate than forecast is **soft**, because it means a weaker jobs market."
widget = "surprise_meter"
why = "In line means close to the forecast; the market's plans barely change. How big a miss counts as a surprise depends on the release; this course keeps it simple: above, below or in line."

[[steps]]
heading = "Which way gold usually goes"
text = "Hot data makes traders expect the Fed to keep rates higher, so yields and the dollar tend to rise, and gold **usually falls**. Soft data points to lower rates, so gold **usually rises**. That's the chain from Lessons 16 and 17."
why = "The chain: hot data → expect higher rates → higher yields and a stronger dollar → higher opportunity cost for gold → gold down. Soft data runs it backwards."

[[steps]]
heading = "Why it can go the other way"
text = "A headline can be hot while the details are soft, last month's figure can be revised, or the surprise was smaller than traders feared. Then gold can do the **opposite** of the textbook reaction, sometimes within minutes."
why = "That's why this course teaches the reaction as a tendency to recognise, not a trade to take. You'll see more of this in the Advanced Track's News Follow-Through lesson, after Live Readiness."

[[steps]]
heading = "Don't trade the release candle"
text = "In the first seconds after a tier-1 release, spreads widen, stops slip and price often whipsaws both ways. Your **No-Trade Window** keeps you out for 5 minutes either side. Afterwards, trade only your normal setup at a level, with a stop."
why = "Trading the release is a gamble on direction with the worst costs of the day. Waiting costs you nothing; your plan's setup is still there afterwards."
