[[steps]]
heading = "Support and resistance"
text = "**Support** is a price area where falling price tends to stop or turn up. **Resistance** is where rising price tends to stop or turn down. CME Group's lessons describe them as levels where price is **more likely** to pause or change direction."
why = "They come from memory: traders remember where price turned before and act there again. Support is usually below the current price, resistance above it."

[[steps]]
heading = "Zones, not lines"
text = "Price rarely turns at exactly the same cent. Draw a **zone** a few dollars wide around the turns, covering the wicks and the bodies. With a single exact line, you'd think a level failed when price only poked through it."
why = "This course starts with zones a few dollars wide on H4. If a zone needs to be $20 wide to hold every turn, it's probably two levels, or none."

[[steps]]
heading = "Real after two reactions"
text = "A level is worth trusting once price has **reacted to it at least twice**: turned there, left, came back and turned again. One touch is just a turn. The more clear reactions, the more traders are watching it."
why = "Two reactions is this course's rule of thumb, not a law of the market. Lesson 28 trades exactly this: a floor or ceiling price has already respected twice."

[[steps]]
heading = "Round numbers"
text = "Prices like **4,100.00** or **4,050.00** often act as levels too, because many traders place orders at them. CME Group shows crude oil failing for years to break through $100. Treat gold's $50 and $100 steps as places to watch, not walls."
why = "A round number that is also an old turning point is stronger than either alone. Stage 6 calls that confluence."

[[steps]]
heading = "Broken levels flip"
text = "When price breaks through support and stays below, that zone often becomes **resistance** on the way back up. Broken resistance often becomes support. CME Group describes this role reversal, which is why a broken level stays on your chart."
why = "Price often comes back to test a broken level from the other side before moving on. That retest is a setup of its own in the Advanced Track (breakout and retest)."

[[steps]]
heading = "Trade the reaction, not the level"
text = "A zone tells you **where** to pay attention, not what to do. Act only on a reaction there: price slowing, a confirmation candle, in the higher-timeframe direction, with a stop beyond the zone."
why = "Levels break regularly. The stop beyond the zone is what turns a failed level into a small, planned loss instead of a hope."
