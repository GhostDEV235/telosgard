heading = "Why scheduled news moves gold"
text = "Inflation and jobs numbers change what traders expect the **Fed** to do with interest rates, and rates move gold (Lesson 17). So when a big US number comes out, gold reprices in seconds. The biggest of these releases are called **tier 1**."
why = "“Tier” is just a label for importance. Economic calendars mark it with colours or stars. This course uses three tiers: tier 1 always gets a No-Trade Window; tiers 2 and 3 are noted in your checklist."

[[steps]]
heading = "CPI: the inflation number"
text = "The **Consumer Price Index (CPI)** measures how fast prices are rising for US households. It's published monthly by the Bureau of Labor Statistics at **8:30 am New York time**. Higher-than-expected inflation can mean higher rates for longer."
why = "Traders often watch “core” CPI too, which leaves out food and energy because they jump around. The release shows the change on the month and on the year."

[[steps]]
heading = "NFP: the jobs report"
text = "The **jobs report** (the Employment Situation, nicknamed **NFP** for non-farm payrolls) shows how many jobs the US added last month and the unemployment rate. It usually comes out on the **first Friday of the month at 8:30 am** New York time."
why = "A strong jobs market can push inflation up and keep the Fed cautious about cutting rates. A weak one can do the opposite. The BLS publishes the exact dates in its release calendar; your checklist links to it."

[[steps]]
heading = "FOMC: the Fed's decision"
text = "Eight times a year the Fed's **FOMC** announces its interest-rate decision at **2:00 pm New York time**, and the Fed Chair holds a press conference about **30 minutes later**. Gold can move on both: the decision, and what the Chair says next."
why = "Four of the eight meetings also publish the Summary of Economic Projections, where officials show where they expect rates to go. Those meetings often move markets more."

[[steps]]
heading = "Tier 2, and reading the calendar"

why = "An economic calendar shows each release with its time in your time zone, the previous value, the forecast and, once out, the actual number. Lesson 19 is about comparing those last two."
