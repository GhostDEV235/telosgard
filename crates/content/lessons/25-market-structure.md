[[steps]]
heading = "Higher highs and higher lows"
text = "In an **uptrend**, each swing high is higher than the last (**HH**) and each swing low is higher too (**HL**). In a **downtrend**: lower highs (**LH**) and lower lows (**LL**). Lesson 23 called these up and down; now they have names."
why = "CME Group's reversal lesson describes a market that couldn't make a higher high on its second try as one that may be running out of momentum. Structure is how you see that happening."

[[steps]]
heading = "Ranges"
text = "When the highs and lows are roughly level, neither side is winning: it's a **range**. Price swings between a ceiling and a floor, Lesson 24's zones. In a range there's no trend to follow; the edges are the levels that matter."
why = "Ranges are common on gold, especially during the quieter Asian session (Lesson 14)."

[[steps]]
heading = "BOS: the trend continues"
text = "A **break of structure (BOS)** is price breaking the last swing in the trend's own direction: above the last high in an uptrend, below the last low in a downtrend. It confirms the trend is still going."
why = "Use the candle's close: a wick that pokes through and closes back inside is not a clean break."

[[steps]]
heading = "CHoCH: the trend may be turning"
text = "A **change of character (CHoCH)** is price breaking the last swing **against** the trend: below the last higher low in an uptrend, or above the last lower high in a downtrend. It's the first warning that the trend may be ending."
why = "BOS and CHoCH are traders' shorthand, popular in Smart Money Concepts (Advanced Track). The idea underneath is plain market structure."

[[steps]]
heading = "A warning, not a reversal"
text = "One CHoCH doesn't make a new trend. Treat it as a reason to stop trading the old direction and wait. A new downtrend needs its own lower high and lower low. Until then the picture is unclear, and unclear means stand aside."
why = "Many CHoCHs fail: price dips below a higher low, then the uptrend carries on. Waiting for the new structure costs a little of the move and saves many false starts."
